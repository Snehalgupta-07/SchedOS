=== Architectural Trade-off Analysis (Operating System Principles) ===
1. First-Come, First-Served (FCFS):
   - Pros: Minimal scheduling overhead (0 preemption); starvation-free.
   - Cons: Susceptible to Convoy Effect; higher average turnaround time when long bursts arrive early.

2. Shortest Job First (SJF - Non-Preemptive):
   - Pros: Mathematically provable minimum average waiting time for static batch arrivals.
   - Cons: Potential starvation for long-running jobs; requires burst-time knowledge.

3. Shortest Remaining Time First (SRTF - Preemptive):
   - Pros: Highly responsive to newly arrived short bursts; reduces average turnaround time dynamically.
   - Cons: High context-switching overhead under frequent arrivals; risk of starvation.

4. Round Robin (RR - Preemptive):
   - Pros: Optimal for interactive time-sharing systems; guaranteed bounded response time.
   - Cons: Overhead highly sensitive to quantum size 'q' (too small = excessive CS, too large = degrades to FCFS).

5. Priority Scheduling (Non-Preemptive):
   - Pros: Directly aligns CPU allocation with real-world task criticalities.
   - Cons: Lower-priority jobs face indefinite postponement (requires aging techniques in production).

Summary: No single scheduling algorithm is universally superior. Production OS kernels
(e.g., Linux CFS or Siemens industrial RTOS) combine these strategies into multi-level priority queues.
